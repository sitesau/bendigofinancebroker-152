Comparing Finance Structures

The service mentions chattel mortgage, finance lease, and hire purchase as potential equipment finance structures, alongside the option of balloon payments for asset finance. The choice of structure should extend beyond just the monthly repayment amount, considering factors like ownership, Goods and Services Tax (GST) implications, depreciation allowances, end-of-term arrangements, and advice from an accountant.

A chattel mortgage typically means the borrower owns the asset from the outset, allowing for upfront GST claims and potential depreciation deductions. With a finance lease, the lender retains ownership of the asset while the borrower leases it; GST is applied to the repayments, and the borrower may have the option to purchase or return the asset at the lease's conclusion.

Given the page's recommendation to consult an accountant, the most practical approach is to first identify suitable finance structures and then verify their tax treatment with a qualified accountant before finalising any agreement. This ensures the chosen structure is not selected solely based on a lower initial repayment figure.
